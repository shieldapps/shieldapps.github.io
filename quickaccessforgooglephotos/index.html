Quick Access for Google Photos

Native macOS companion for Google Photos: open your library in one place, then upload from anywhere on your Mac—without hunting for a browser tab.

⚡ Quick access

Stay signed in and jump straight into Google Photos from a dedicated window: familiar web experience with a native toolbar, multi-account switching, and a menu-bar presence so your library is always within reach.

📤 Quick upload

Add photos and videos through native uploads with queue & progress: drag files to the Dock icon, use the Share menu, run a Finder Service, or pick files from the menu bar—ideal when the fastest path is “send it to Photos now.”

What you can do

🖼️ Browse Google Photos in-app

Familiar photos.google.com experience

  • Use Google Photos in a dedicated window with back, forward, reload, and Home
  • Upload and organize using the same web UI you already know
  • Support for saving downloads where macOS allows (standard web downloads)
  • Appearance tuned for macOS, including dark mode styling assistance

📤 Quick upload from the system

Shortcuts that skip the browser

  • Dock: drag photos or videos onto the app icon (including multiple files)
  • Share extension: send items from Photos, Finder, or other apps into the upload flow
  • Finder Services: upload via the Services menu (e.g. contextual workflow)
  • Menu bar: add files from an upload picker without opening the main window first
  • Batched queue with overall progress, pause/resume, and persisted state across sessions

📊 Menu bar status & queue

Always-visible control

  • Status item shows when uploads are active or need attention
  • Popover lists batches and per-file status
  • Open the main Google Photos window or preferences from the menu bar
  • Closing the main window does not have to stop background uploads

👥 Multiple Google accounts

  • Add more than one Google account and switch from the toolbar
  • Separate web session data per account
  • Upload queues are scoped per account—switching accounts follows in-app safeguards

🔐 Privacy-minded design

  • OAuth 2.0 sign-in; tokens stored in the macOS Keychain
  • No Shield Apps server in the middle for your library: API calls go to Google
  • Limited Photos Library API scope for native uploads (photoslibrary.appendonly)

💎 Pro options

  • Subscription or one-time purchase may unlock extended usage (see in-app details on the Mac App Store)
  • Restore purchases supported through Apple’s standard flow

How it works

1. Sign in securely

Connect your Google account with OAuth. The app never sees your Google password.

2. Quick access

Open Google Photos in the main window whenever you want a full browsing session—toolbar controls and account switching stay native.

3. Quick upload

Drop files on the Dock icon, Share to the app, use Services, or choose files from the menu bar. Track everything in the upload popover.

4. Keep working

Leave the window closed if you like—the menu bar keeps uploads visible until they finish.

System requirements

  • macOS 12 (Monterey) or later recommended
  • Internet connection
  • Google account with Google Photos

FAQ

What is the difference between uploads in the web view and “quick upload”?

The in-app browser uses Google’s own Photos website—you can upload there as you would in Safari. Quick upload is the native pipeline: it talks to the Google Photos Library API, shows a single queue in the menu bar, supports pausing, and is designed for system-wide shortcuts (Dock, Share, Services, file picker).

Is my content sent through your servers?

No. Native uploads and API calls go between your Mac and Google’s services. We do not operate a backend that stores your photo library. See the Privacy Policy for details on local storage (tokens, queue metadata, web data).

Does Google endorse this app?

No. Quick Access for Google Photos is an independent product from Shield Apps. Google Photos and related marks belong to Google LLC.

Can I use multiple accounts?

Yes. Add accounts from the account menu in the toolbar and switch between them with confirmation when uploads are in progress.

Will uploads continue if I close the window?

Native quick uploads are designed to keep running with the app in the background; use the menu bar popover to monitor progress. Web-based uploads inside Google’s site follow Google’s own behavior in the embedded browser.

Any limitations on downloads?

Downloads depend on how Google Photos serves each action in the browser. Some flows (for example certain multi-item or archive-style downloads) may behave differently from single-item saves. If something does not save, try downloading fewer items at once or use Google’s official clients as a fallback.
